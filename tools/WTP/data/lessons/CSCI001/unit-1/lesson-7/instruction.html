Tutorial

For a conversion student, the Experience and Education section is particularly important. It tells the story of how you arrived here, whether through a previous degree, work, or both, and shows an employer what you bring that a typical computing graduate does not. In this lesson you will present it as a timeline.


Presenting a History

A CV lists your history in reverse chronological order: most recent first. Your portfolio should do the same, because a visitor is most interested in what you are doing now. A timeline adds a visual line down the side, which makes the sequence easy to follow at a glance.

For each entry, include:

  • The dates, so the order is clear
  • The role or qualification, as the entry's heading
  • The organisation and one key achievement. Lead with what you achieved rather than a list of duties, and include a number if you can: "Reduced weekly reporting time by 30%" is far more memorable than "Responsible for reporting".

This is a summary, not your whole CV. Three or four entries is usually enough, and you can link to your full CV or LinkedIn profile for the details.


Building the Component

Each entry uses the same structure, just like the project cards:

<div class="timeline">
    <div class="entry">
        <p class="date">2026 – present</p>
        <h3>MSc Computing</h3>
        <p>University of Law. Studying software development and databases.</p>
    </div>
</div>

<div> is a general-purpose container with no meaning of its own. It is used purely to group elements so they can be styled together. Use a semantic element when one fits; use a div when you just need a box.


Drawing the Line

The timeline's line is simply a left border on the container:

.timeline {
    border-left: 3px solid #94248C;
    padding-left: 20px;
}

The padding-left pushes the entries away from the line so the text does not touch it. Small styling choices like this, such as making the dates smaller and coloured, create a visual hierarchy: the reader's eye goes to the most important information first.


Your Task

  1. The first entry is done for you. Add at least two more <div class="entry"> elements for your previous qualifications and any work or volunteering, most recent first.
  2. Write each one with a date, a heading and one achievement.
  3. Complete the .timeline, .entry, .date and .entry h3 rules in the CSS tab.

Extension Tasks

  • Add a small circle on the line for each entry. Give .entry the rule position: relative;, then use .entry::before with content: "";, a width and height of 12px, a background colour, border-radius: 50%, position: absolute; and left: -28px;. This is your first taste of positioning, which you will cover properly in Web Technologies.
  • Rewrite one of your entries so it leads with an achievement and includes a number.

Recap

  • List experience most recent first, and lead with achievements.
  • div is a general-purpose container for grouping and styling.
  • A simple border can create a strong visual effect such as a timeline.
